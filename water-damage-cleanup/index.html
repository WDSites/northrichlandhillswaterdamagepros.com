Emergency Water Damage Cleanup in North Richland Hills, TX

Once the water is stopped, the cleanup decides whether the house smells, stays damp or grows mold. Our crews remove what cannot be saved and sanitize the rest.

Water is stopped, but the house is still a mess: what cleanup covers

If water entered your home within the last 48 hours, cleanup is time-sensitive, and our line is open 24/7. While you wait, keep children and pets out of wet rooms, open windows if the weather allows, wear gloves and boots around dirty water and set aside anything you want a crew to inspect rather than throw away.

Water damage cleanup is the removal of water, debris and damaged materials, followed by cleaning, sanitizing and odor control. It follows water mitigation, which stops the spread and dries the structure, and it comes before the rebuild. The goal is a clean, dry shell that is ready for repair.

Cleanup follows the water categories described in ANSI/IICRC S500, the published standard for professional water damage restoration.

The cleanup sequence, in order

  1. Sort and protect. Contents are separated into salvageable, questionable and disposable, and valuables are moved to dry areas.
  2. Remove what cannot be saved. Wet pad, soaked insulation, swollen drywall and contaminated materials leave the house in bags.
  3. Clean surfaces. Floors, studs and salvageable finishes are cleaned of silt, residue and staining.
  4. Sanitize. Antimicrobial treatment goes on surfaces that touched anything beyond clean supply water, as the water category requires.
  5. Control odor. Deodorizing follows the removal, because covering odors before the source is gone does not work.
  6. Verify. Moisture readings confirm the area is dry enough to rebuild.

The first 48 hours matter most

Water damage cleanup carries urgency because damp materials turn into mold food quickly. The EPA advises drying wet materials within 24 to 48 hours to keep mold from starting, and many cleanups are about beating that clock.

That is why we ask callers to describe how long water has been in the house. A room that was wet this morning needs different work than one where carpet has been damp since last weekend. We also ask what the water was: a supply line, a washing machine, a toilet or a drain.

Contents, odors and what homeowners can handle

Hard, nonporous items such as glass, metal and sealed plastics can usually be washed and returned. Upholstered furniture, mattresses and paper items from dirty water are rarely worth keeping. Photograph anything you plan to claim before it is thrown out.

Odors tell you something is still wet or contaminated. If a musty smell returns after cleaning, ask the crew to recheck the wall cavity and subfloor, because that smell usually comes from moisture left behind. If you see growth, the mold remediation page explains what changes.

For your own protection, wear gloves, boots and a mask when handling wet debris, and do not mix cleaning products. If you smell gas or see damage near electrical equipment, leave and call the utility or 911.

What affects the cost, and what insurance may do

Cleanup cost follows the scope. A few facts move it more than any others.

  • The water category and how long the water sat.
  • Affected square footage and number of rooms.
  • How much carpet, pad, insulation and drywall has to be removed and hauled away.
  • Contents handling and how much sanitizing the category requires.
  • How many drying days or follow-up visits readings call for.
