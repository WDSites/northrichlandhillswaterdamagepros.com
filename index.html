Water damage restoration for North Richland Hills homes

A burst supply line, a failed water heater, a roof leak after a storm or a sewer backup all begin the same way: water is somewhere it should not be, and every hour it sits does more damage. Water damage restoration is the work of stopping that damage, which means removing standing water, drying the structure, cleaning what can be saved and rebuilding what cannot.

We dispatch crews to addresses in 76180 and 76182 and to nearby towns, with an emergency line that answers 24/7. Our crews take moisture readings, explain the scope and price before work starts and keep records you can give to your insurer. If mold follows a leak, the same crews can handle it. Call (817) 623-9267 any hour.

Industry practice for this work is published in ANSI/IICRC S500 for water damage and S520 for mold, and you can ask any company whether its crews follow them.

What to have ready for your insurance claim

Large losses involve insurers, and a clear record helps. Photograph each wet room before anything is moved, note when you first saw the water and keep any failed part. Our crews add moisture readings and a written scope so the file shows what was found and what was done.

Coverage depends on your policy and on where the water came from, so ask your insurer what applies. A sudden pipe failure, a slow leak, a sewer backup and creek water are often treated differently.

  • Wide and close photos of every wet room.
  • The date and time you noticed the water and shut off the source.
  • The failed part, such as a cracked supply line, if it is safe to keep.
  • A list of damaged belongings with rough ages.
  • The scope and price, in writing, before demolition starts.

What North Richland Hills homes are up against

North Richland Hills was incorporated in 1953 after Clarence Jones began subdividing his dairy farm near what is now Cummings Drive in 1952. Smithfield was annexed by 1960. Houses in those older areas now carry supply lines and water heaters that are 50 years old or more, so a crew that arrives for one leak often checks the neighboring walls too.

Weather adds its own load. The National Weather Service lists 37.01 inches of normal annual rain and an average of 29 freezes a season at the Dallas/Fort Worth station. In February 2021 Fort Worth reported 655 water main breaks during the winter storm, a reminder of what a hard freeze can do to pipes.

For flooding questions, the city offers an interactive floodplain map and Public Works Engineering can issue a floodplain determination letter at 817-427-6400. The city also notes that its flood maps show creek and channel flooding, and not poor lot grading, which can put water in a garage outside the mapped floodplain. Crews reach homes by Davis Boulevard, Mid Cities Boulevard and Interstate 820, and ask your water utility or the city building department about repairs that may need a permit.
