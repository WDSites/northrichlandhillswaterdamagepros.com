How commercial water damage restoration works here

Water in a commercial property is handled the same way as water in a house. We extract standing water, survey the materials with meters and a thermal camera, dry the structure with air movers and dehumidifiers and clean what the water touched. The published standard ANSI/IICRC S500 is the reference for that work, and nothing about the method changes, only the footprint it is applied to.

What does change is the size of the question. A dispatcher scopes the loss on the call, asking how much of the property is wet, where the water started and whether it has stopped. That tells the crew what equipment to bring. Pipe, roof and equipment repairs stay with the plumber, roofer or HVAC technician.

What you see and what it tells you

Signs are familiar. Stained or sagging ceiling tiles, wet carpet that spreads across a floor, a musty smell, bubbling paint and puddles under a wall all mean moisture is present.

In a larger space the wet area is often wider than the visible stain. Water travels under flooring and along walls, so a dry-looking corner can still be above wet material. Report what you see to the dispatcher in as much detail as you can.

Safety steps in the first hour

The first hour is about stopping the water and keeping people safe. If anyone is in immediate danger, call 911 first.

  • Shut off the water at the valve if you know where it is, and note the time.
  • Cut power to a wet area only when you can reach the switch without stepping in water.
  • Keep people away from sagging ceilings, slick floors and wet cords.
  • Tell the owner or property manager, and notify your insurer or agent.
  • Photograph the damage first, then move items.

Who to bring in and what to document

Several parties may need to know quickly: the owner, the property manager and the insurer. If the space is leased, the lease generally decides who is responsible for what, so check the lease and ask the owner or manager before assuming.

Commercial insurance differs from the homeowners policies the Texas Department of Insurance describes, so ask your agent how your policy treats water. We never promise coverage. Keep a record of what happened, photos, the time the water was noticed and receipts for emergency steps.

How the process scales and what we do on site

A larger footprint means more equipment and more monitoring. More air movers and dehumidifiers go in, readings are taken in more places and the drying log covers more rooms.

The plan for working around people who are using the space is discussed on the call. Our 24/7 line is the way to reach us, and we do not promise after-hours work beyond that. For the full drying approach, see structural drying, and for the first days, see water mitigation.

North Richland Hills notes: permits, floodplain and city systems

The city website links to an E-Services portal for permits and inspections and posts the Code of Ordinances online. Structural and permit questions belong with the city building department, not with a restoration crew.

The city floodplain FAQ says a floodplain determination letter comes from Public Works Engineering, reached at 817-427-6400.

Per the city utility page, Public Works looks after roughly 379 miles of water main and 292 miles of sewer line. The city drainage page adds that storm water moves through the municipal system to creeks and channels. If outside water is involved, see flood damage restoration. What changes the cost of commercial water damage

  • The size of the wet footprint and how many rooms or areas are involved.
  • How long the water sat and what it soaked.
  • The materials affected, such as flooring, wall board, ceiling and insulation.
  • How much equipment and monitoring the area needs.
  • Whether the work has to be arranged around people using the space.

Before work begins, a dispatcher goes through scope and price when you call (817) 623-9267.

Insurance, the lease and mistakes to avoid

Coverage turns on the cause of the water and the terms of your commercial policy, so ask your agent early and check the lease. We never promise coverage.

Avoid waiting for an adjuster before stopping the water, discarding damaged material before photos and assuming a dry surface means a dry structure. Call (817) 623-9267 when the water is found.
