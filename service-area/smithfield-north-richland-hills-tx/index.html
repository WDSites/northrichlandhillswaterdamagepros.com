Water Damage Restoration in Smithfield, North Richland Hills, TX

Smithfield is one of the oldest communities in North Richland Hills, so homes of several ages sit side by side.

An old community inside a newer city

Smithfield predates North Richland Hills. A local history source records the plat of the old town of Smithfield on October 23, 1888, and the city history page says Smithfield was annexed into North Richland Hills in 1960.

Today the area around Smithfield Road north of Mid Cities Boulevard includes a TEX Rail station at 6416 Smithfield Road. In a community with a long history, a crew can meet very different plumbing, flooring and wall construction from one house to the next. Call (817) 623-9267 with the details.

Facts we can point to

The Smithfield Station profile from the North Central Texas Council of Governments describes a park-and-ride with 580 spaces on the TEX Rail line. Within a half mile of the station it counted 5,704 housing units, about 76 percent owner-occupied and 24 percent renter-occupied. The page was last updated in 2016, so treat the numbers as a snapshot.

None of these sources says Smithfield homes suffer more water damage, and older age alone does not prove it. Neighborhood-specific FEMA flood-zone status not verified for Smithfield. For the floodplain status of one address, the city points residents to its interactive map or a determination from Public Works Engineering.

Safety in the first hour

Older wiring, outlets near sinks and ceiling fixtures can all be in the path of leaking water. Go slowly and stay on the safe side. Here is what to do while you wait.

  • Assume older outlets and light fixtures near a leak are live until an electrician says otherwise.
  • Turn off the main water valve if it is safe to reach, then open a faucet at the lowest point to drain lines.
  • Never run a wet vacuum or fan on an extension cord across a wet floor.
  • Record the room, the date and the source in photos or a short video.
  • Do not scrape, sand or cut old plaster, tile or flooring that is wet, because it may hold hazardous materials.
  • If you smell gas or see sparks, leave and call 911, then call (817) 623-9267 for the water.

Extraction, drying and repair

The crew extracts standing water, maps the moisture and sets air movers and dehumidifiers for the structure. Wet ceiling drywall and insulation are often opened early because they hold water. Repairs follow only after the readings say the area is dry.

ANSI/IICRC S500 is the published industry reference for the process. If you suspect asbestos or lead in an older material, tell the crew before anything is cut, and do not disturb it yourself. We note materials as they are found.

Insurance and landlord notice

Cause decides coverage. State insurance guidance treats a sudden water event differently from a long-running leak, and reporting deadlines vary by policy, so read yours and notify your insurer early.

Renters should also tell the landlord at once. Photos, moisture readings and an itemized scope can help describe the loss. They do not promise a payment.
