Water Damage Restoration in HomeTown, North Richland Hills, TX

HomeTown is a planned mixed-use community with homes close together, so water from one home can reach another.

A planned community near Mid Cities Boulevard

HomeTown is a 287-acre mixed-use development in North Richland Hills between Mid Cities Boulevard and Highway 26, according to the North Central Texas Council of Governments. It was designed to be walkable, with the Lakes at HomeTown, Walker's Creek Park, trail connections, the city library and an elementary school.

A local housing guide describes a mix of townhomes, small-lot single-family houses and traditional homes built in phases. If water has entered yours, reach the dispatcher at (817) 623-9267 and say which type of home you have.

What the planning record says

The council planning page lists a planned mix of 1,000 residential units, 55,000 square feet of retail and 10,000 square feet of office. It does not say when building began, so we do not give a build date. A city story map from September 2023 describes pedestrian oriented development and form-based code in the city's urban villages.

We found no source describing flooding in HomeTown, and we do not claim any. Neighborhood-specific FEMA flood-zone status not verified for HomeTown. Water damage here is more likely to start with plumbing, appliances, roofs and windows than with a named drainage problem.

Safety in the first hour

In attached homes, electrical circuits and plumbing chases can be shared or hidden in walls. Be careful, and do not open a wall near water. Here is what to do while you wait.

  • Knock on a shared wall or ceiling only to alert the neighbor, and do not open it near water or wiring.
  • Find the main shutoff for your unit and close it if the valve is dry and easy to reach.
  • Keep power off to wet rooms, and do not use appliances plugged into wet outlets.
  • Photograph every level the water touched, including closets, garages and stairs.
  • Contact the neighbor, association or landlord early if the water crossed a boundary.
  • Call the utility or 911 for a gas smell or sparks, then call (817) 623-9267 for drying.

Extraction, drying and shared structure

The crew extracts standing water and maps moisture with meters and a thermal camera, including the wall and floor on the other side of a shared boundary. Air movers and dehumidifiers dry the structure, and wet insulation or drywall is removed when it will not dry in place.

The published ANSI/IICRC S500 standard describes the process. Where a loss crosses units, the crew tells you what it found on your side and leaves questions about the neighbor's property to the neighbor, the association or the landlord.

Insurance and who reports what

Insurance guidance from the state treats a sudden water event differently from a continuous leak. Policies and association rules differ, so read yours and ask your insurer who should report a loss that begins next door.

Photos, moisture readings and an itemized scope help describe what happened. They do not decide whether a claim is paid.
