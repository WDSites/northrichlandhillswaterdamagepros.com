How coverage works

Our home base is North Richland Hills, where Public Works maintains about 379 miles of water mains, 2,175 fire hydrants and 292 miles of sewer lines according to the city. That network is why a leak in one neighborhood can look very different from a leak two miles away.

A call to our line does not depend on which town you are in. The dispatcher asks for your address, the source of the water and what it touched, then arranges a crew. If the problem is on the public side, such as a water main or a street drain, the dispatcher will tell you which city office to call.

Rain, freezes and the calls that follow

The National Weather Service lists a normal of 37.01 inches of rain a year at the Dallas/Fort Worth station, along with an average of 29 freezes per season. Heavy rain tests roofs, windows and grading, and hard freezes split supply lines that run through exterior walls and attics.

Those two patterns show up in every town on this page. A homeowner in Keller and one in Hurst may face different drainage, but both depend on knowing where the main shutoff is and who to call when it fails. Tell the dispatcher what you see, and they will explain the first steps over the phone.

After a storm, check ceilings, window frames and the attic for stains, and look at the lowest point of your yard for pooled water. After a freeze, check exposed pipes in the garage and along exterior walls before the thaw.

What to tell us if you are near a city line

Several of these towns share borders, and mailing addresses do not always match city limits. Give the dispatcher your street and cross street, and say which city you believe you are in. They will confirm coverage on the call.

Addresses in Bedford, Birdville and Southlake are close to our main towns, so a call is the quickest way to learn whether a crew can reach you. We would rather say so plainly than guess, and we will never promise a crew we cannot send.

If we cannot cover your address, the dispatcher will say so quickly, so you can call someone else without losing time while water keeps spreading.

If you share a wall with a neighbor, mention that too. Water can move between units, and the crew needs to know whether other homes might be affected.
