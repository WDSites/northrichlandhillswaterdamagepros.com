Water Damage Restoration in Meadow Lakes, North Richland Hills, TX

Meadow Lakes has a creek tributary and two small lakes nearby, so the first question is whether the water came from the plumbing or from outside.

A subdivision built around two small lakes

Meadow Lakes is a residential subdivision in North Richland Hills near Interstate 820. City budget documents place it along Meadow Lakes Drive, Lake Side Circle, Pebble Court and Riviera Drive, with two lakes between Riviera Drive and Meadow Lakes Drive.

Water damage in a neighborhood like this can start in two very different ways. A supply line, water heater or drain can fail inside the house, or rain can push water in from outside. A crew handles each differently, so tell the dispatcher which one you are looking at when you call (817) 623-9267.

What the city has documented near Meadow Lakes

The City of North Richland Hills capital budget describes a flood study of the BFC-7 tributary of Big Fossil Creek. The study covers Meadow Lakes Drive, Lake Side Circle, Pebble Court and the two lakes, and aims to update the FEMA model and move the area from Zone A to Zone AE.

The same budget page says 47 property owners have homes or lots in the Zone A floodplain, and that some homes may sit wholly inside it because the current model is missing data. That describes specific properties, not every house in Meadow Lakes. It also describes a study, not a flood event.

A later city budget lists drainage work to enlarge the storm drain system toward the eastern lake. Neighborhood-specific FEMA flood-zone status not verified for Meadow Lakes. The city suggests its interactive map or a floodplain determination from Public Works Engineering to check one address.

Safety in the first hour

Water in a house can carry electrical, slipping and contamination hazards. Water that came in from outside should be treated as contaminated until a professional says otherwise. Here is what to do first.

  • Stay out of any room where water has reached outlets, cords or the panel.
  • Shut the main water valve only when you can reach it on dry footing.
  • Keep fans, shop vacuums and extension cords out of standing water.
  • Photograph the damage and the water line once the room is safe to enter.
  • Treat water from a yard, street or drain as contaminated, and keep children and pets away.
  • Call the utility or 911 for a gas smell or sparks, then call (817) 623-9267 about the water.

How extraction and drying work

A crew removes standing water first with pumps and extraction units. Moisture meters and a thermal camera then map where the water traveled, because wet drywall, insulation and subfloor often hide behind a dry surface.

Air movers and dehumidifiers dry the structure, and readings are repeated until the materials reach their dry standard. Materials that cannot be dried safely are removed. ANSI/IICRC S500 is the published industry reference for this kind of work. Fans and a household vacuum are not enough for water that came from a drain or from outside.

Insurance and documentation

In its consumer guidance, the Texas Department of Insurance notes that flood damage is usually left out of home and renters policies, with a few exceptions. A burst pipe is the kind of sudden event most policies address, while a long-running leak is typically excluded. Sewer or drain backup needs an added endorsement.

That means the cause matters. Photos, moisture readings and an itemized scope can help describe what was found and what was done. Whether your policy responds depends on its terms, so read it and ask your insurer.
