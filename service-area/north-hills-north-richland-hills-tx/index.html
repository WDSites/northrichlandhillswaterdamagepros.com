Water Damage Restoration in North Hills, North Richland Hills, TX

Part of North Hills was developed without underground drainage, so runoff and indoor leaks both deserve a close look.

The North Hills subdivision

North Hills is a residential subdivision in North Richland Hills, known to the city as the North Hills Addition. The part with the best documentation lies along Bewley Drive. The name can be confused with Medical City North Hills, which is a hospital, so this page is about the neighborhood.

When water gets into a house here, the useful first question is where it started. A burst supply line, a failed water heater and a gutter or yard that sends runoff toward the wall each leave different damage. Ring (817) 623-9267 and describe to the dispatcher what you see.

What the city says about drainage on Bewley Drive

A City of North Richland Hills capital budget describes the North Hills Addition Bewley Drive Drainage Improvements project. It says a portion of the subdivision was developed decades ago without underground drainage.

The budget says the project addresses property damage from flooding where stormwater runoff leaves an unimproved drainage easement. It planned about 450 linear feet of concrete flume with grouted rock riprap at the end to prevent erosion. Later budgets repeat that the area was built without underground drainage.

Those pages describe a project proposed in 2020, so ask Public Works about its current status. The city also says flood maps cover creeks and channels, not undersized drainage systems or poor lot grading. Neighborhood-specific FEMA flood-zone status not verified for North Hills.

Safety in the first hour

Water that entered from a yard or street may carry soil, debris and bacteria. Treat it with care until it has been assessed. Here is what to do and what to leave alone.

  • Do not wade through a wet garage or low room if an outlet or extension cord sits in the water.
  • Stop the supply at the main valve only if the valve is dry and easy to reach.
  • Leave the breaker box alone if the floor around it is wet, and call the utility for help.
  • Take wide photos of the yard, the wall and the floor before anything is moved.
  • Move shoes, boxes and small electronics off wet floors, and leave heavy items where they are.
  • For sparks or a gas smell, call the utility or 911, then call (817) 623-9267 for removal and drying.

Extraction, drying and what gets removed

A crew pumps or extracts standing water, then uses meters and a thermal camera to find wet drywall, insulation, baseboard and subfloor. Air movers and dehumidifiers dry what can be saved. Carpet pad and soaked insulation are often removed rather than dried.

The aim is a dry structure that reads dry on the meters, not a house that only feels dry. The ANSI/IICRC S500 standard is the reference most professionals use for this work. Mold is a separate concern, covered on the mold remediation page.

Talking to your insurer

Cause and policy wording decide what is covered. Texas Department of Insurance guidance separates sudden water events, which most policies address, from a continuous leak and from flood, which are usually excluded.

Document the damage, notify your agent or company early and keep damaged items until you have talked to them. A photo set and moisture readings can help describe the loss. We cannot say whether a claim will be paid.
