How to Remove Mold From a Wall in North Richland Hills, TX

Small spots on a sealed surface can be wiped, but growth in soft drywall or inside the wall cavity needs a different plan.

By the North Richland Hills Water Damage Pros team. Published October 26, 2026.

Surface growth or growth inside the wall?

You can reasonably wipe a small spot on a hard, sealed surface such as glossy trim, tile or grout, using detergent and water, then drying it fully. Painted drywall is a different case. Its paper face absorbs moisture, so growth that returns after cleaning, or drywall that feels soft, points to a problem inside the wall.

The rule that matters most is the source. Mold needs moisture, so a leak or a damp spot has to be fixed before any cleaning counts for much. Wiping a wall that is still wet behind the surface changes how it looks and nothing else.

Location gives clues too. Growth on an outside wall in a closet, behind a headboard or behind a sofa often points to cool surfaces and poor airflow. Growth under a window points to a seal or flashing problem, and growth near a bathroom points to steam and a slow plumbing leak.

What to check before you touch the wall

A few minutes of looking tells you which situation you have. Use a flashlight, and press gently on the wall with a gloved finger rather than scraping it.

  • Look for the moisture source: a shower valve, window edge, supply line, drain or roof leak near the spot.
  • Press the drywall. Soft, crumbly or swollen board means the damage is below the paint.
  • Watch for paint that bubbles, peels or shows rings that look like growth stains.
  • Note any musty smell that stays after the room airs out.
  • Check the other side, such as the closet behind a bathroom wall or the back of a large dresser.
  • Photograph everything before you clean, in case insurance or a repair estimate comes up.

If you find more than one of these signs, assume the wall needs to be opened. A bathroom corner that keeps returning is a classic example, since the cause is usually a moisture problem the surface cleaning never touched.

Why paint over mold fails

Paint hides color, and it does nothing about the moisture or the organic material underneath. Stain-blocking primers can cover a spot for a while, but the wall is still wet or still absorbing humidity. The film bubbles, peels or stains through again.

There is also a practical cost. A painted-over wall is harder to inspect, and anyone who buys the house later or an inspector will find it. Opening the wall, drying it and repairing it properly leaves a record you can show.

If you see a ring of new paint over an old stain, treat it as a sign the problem was handled cosmetically. Press on it, check it with a meter if you can borrow one and ask what happened before you paint again.

What a crew uses to find the real extent

Crews start with a moisture meter, which reads how wet drywall, wood and plaster are, either with pins or by scanning the surface. A thermal camera adds a picture, since damp areas often show as cooler patches. Neither tool names mold, but together they show where water is and how far it traveled. Readings are written down at the start, so the crew can compare them after drying.

When the wall has to come open, the crew seals the area with plastic, runs a HEPA air scrubber and cuts out the damaged drywall, usually past the visible edge of the stain. Framing is cleaned and dried before anything goes back. Mold work follows the ANSI/IICRC S520 standard.

After the wall is opened

Once the damaged drywall is out and the cavity is dry, the rebuild is ordinary carpentry: new board, tape, texture and paint. Water damage repair covers that stage, and it should start only after readings confirm the framing is dry. If the cause was a leak that soaked more than one room, water mitigation explains the drying that comes first.

The same steps apply for homeowners in Haltom City and Colleyville, since a wet wall behaves the same everywhere. A crew can help in either town, and the written scope should name the wall, the cavity and the finish work.
