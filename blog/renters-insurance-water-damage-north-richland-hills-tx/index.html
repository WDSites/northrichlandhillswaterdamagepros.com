Renters Insurance and Water Damage in North Richland Hills, TX

A tenant guide to which water losses a renters policy tends to answer, which ones belong to the landlord, and what to do in the first hour.

By the North Richland Hills Water Damage Pros team. Published October 26, 2026.

Does renters insurance pay for water damage? Often, but the cause decides

Renters insurance often covers your belongings after sudden water damage, such as a burst supply line, an overflowing appliance or a leak from the unit above. It usually does not cover flood, and sewer backup typically needs a separate add-on. Only your policy wording settles each case, so find the declarations page and read the exclusions before you need them.

The Texas Department of Insurance says most home and renters policies do not cover flood damage, although a few do. If water came in from outside, ask your insurer whether you have any flood coverage at all.

Renters face a second question that owners do not. The walls, floors, ceiling and water heater usually belong to the landlord, so your first call is often to the landlord or property manager and your second is to your own insurer. Ask any restoration company which published standard it follows, such as ANSI/IICRC S500 for water damage.

Notice how often the answer starts with "sudden." A slow drip under a sink that went unreported for weeks is the kind of loss insurers argue about. Tell your landlord in writing the day you see a stain, even if it looks small.

Landlord policy versus your policy

The landlord's insurance generally protects the building itself, meaning the structure, the drywall, the flooring that came with the unit and the plumbing. Your policy generally protects what you own, such as furniture, clothing, electronics and bedding. Many renters policies also include personal liability, which matters if your own overflowing tub soaks the neighbor below.

Your lease may add details, including who must report leaks and who arranges repairs. Read that section now, because a landlord who is slow to respond can turn a small leak into a larger loss for everyone in the building.

If the landlord denies that a leak is their problem, ask for that answer in writing and send your insurer a copy. A written record protects you whichever policy ends up paying.

The first hour for a renter

Safety comes first. If water has reached outlets, light fixtures or a breaker panel, stay out of the room and ask the landlord or utility to cut power. If the water is from a drain backup, treat it as contaminated.

Then protect the evidence and the property. Work through the list below while the source is being stopped.

  • Shut off the water at the fixture valve or the unit shutoff if you know where it is, or tell the landlord to shut off the building supply.
  • Photograph and video every wet surface, including the ceiling above, the floor and the baseboards, before you move anything.
  • Lift valuables off wet floors and move them to a dry room, but keep soaked items until your insurer says they can be thrown out.
  • Message the landlord or property manager in writing so the time of your report is on record.
  • Call your renters insurer and ask for the claim number, then write down the date and the name of the person you spoke with.
  • Save receipts for anything you buy because of the loss, such as fans, storage bins or a hotel night.

Photos of a damaged laptop or couch are useful, but so is a list. Write down what each item was, when you bought it and roughly what it cost, while your memory is fresh.

Who pays for drying, and who decides who calls the crew

Drying the building is usually the landlord's job, since the structure is theirs, and many property managers already have a restoration vendor they call. Your policy may help with your own belongings, such as cleaning or replacing contents. If you are unsure which side covers a given step, ask both parties before you sign anything.

Do not authorize work on a building you do not own without the landlord's approval. A tenant can still call us to understand what emergency water removal involves and what the crew would document. We can dispatch a crew once the person who controls the property agrees.

Speed matters because wet drywall, carpet pad and insulation start to break down within days. The water mitigation page walks through the drying sequence, which is the same whether the landlord or a tenant starts the call.

North Richland Hills renters and the flood question

North Richland Hills says homeowners inside and outside floodplains can have drainage problems, and that its flood maps cover creeks and channels but not undersized drainage or poor lot grading. The city also says more than 20 percent of National Flood Insurance Program claims come from properties outside the floodplain.

For a renter, the consequence is simple. A ground-floor unit can take on water even when the address looks safe on a map, and the Texas Department of Insurance says most renters policies leave flood out. If you rent on the ground floor, ask your agent today whether a flood policy for contents is available to you.

The city lists an interactive map and a floodplain determination letter through Public Works Engineering. Your landlord is the person who can request the letter for the property, so ask whether one exists.
