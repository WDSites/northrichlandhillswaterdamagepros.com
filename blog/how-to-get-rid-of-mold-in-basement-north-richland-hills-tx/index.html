How to Get Rid of Mold in a Basement in North Richland Hills, TX

Find the water first, wipe only what is small and hard, and call for containment when mold sits in drywall, carpet or framing.

By the North Richland Hills Water Damage Pros team. Published October 26, 2026.

Find the moisture before you touch the mold

To get rid of mold in a basement, start by finding and stopping the moisture, because mold cannot grow without it. Wiping a stain while a wall wicks groundwater or a pipe drips only gives you a clean spot that turns dark again. Fixing the source comes first, every time.

Walk the lower level with a flashlight and look at where the stains sit. A line along the base of a wall points to water from the slab or the soil. A stain under a bathroom or kitchen points to a plumbing leak, and a ring on a ceiling points to something above.

Check the outside as well. Gutters that dump at the foundation, soil that slopes toward the house and a clogged window well all send water to the same wall.

No basement? The same steps apply

Whether your home has a basement, a crawl space or neither changes where water collects, but not the method. The same steps apply to a garage, a utility closet, a lower-level room or a crawl space, anywhere damp air and building materials meet.

Crawl spaces deserve a note. They are cramped and often hold insulation and wood framing, so a small visible patch can mean a larger hidden area. If you cannot see all of the space safely, a crew can inspect it.

Wipe it yourself or call: a decision guide

Size and material matter more than color. The table separates a job a homeowner can handle from one that needs containment, meaning plastic barriers and controlled airflow so spores do not move into clean rooms.

Wear gloves and eye protection even for a small wipe, and keep the fan pointed outside the house. There is no safe shortcut for anything larger than a small hard surface.

Why bleach does not fix porous material

Drywall, wood, carpet and insulation are porous, which means moisture and growth reach below the surface. A liquid sprayed on top treats what you can see and leaves what is inside. The moisture that fed the mold is also still there, which is the real cause.

A cleaner smell or a whiter surface can feel like success, which is why people spray, paint over and move on. Paint over mold does not remove it, and it hides the early signs that would tell you the leak is back.

Source hunt checklist for a damp lower level

Use this list before you decide what has to be removed. Photograph each finding with a date, because the notes help an insurer, a plumber and a crew.

  • Run a hand along the base of every exterior wall and note any damp or cold spots.
  • Look under sinks, behind the water heater and around the washer for drips or rust stains.
  • Check exterior gutters and downspouts, and note whether the discharge ends at the foundation.
  • Look at the soil next to the house to see if it slopes toward the walls.
  • Inspect window wells for debris, standing water or a cracked seal.
  • Feel the underside of the floor above in a crawl space for dampness or sagging insulation.
  • Run a dehumidifier for a day and see whether the room smells different.

A leaking pipe or a failed seal is a repair for a plumber or contractor. Drying and removing damaged materials is the crew's part, and both have to happen before rebuilding.

What containment and removal look like

A crew that handles mold remediation seals the work area with plastic, controls airflow and removes the affected material before drying and cleaning the structure. Testing and cleanup follow the ANSI/IICRC S520 standard.

Texas licenses mold assessors and remediators through the Texas Department of Licensing and Regulation. Ask the company whether it holds the Texas license that applies to your project, and ask the state how the rules apply to your job.
