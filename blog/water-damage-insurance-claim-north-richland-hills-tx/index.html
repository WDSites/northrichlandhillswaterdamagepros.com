Water Damage Insurance Claim Steps in North Richland Hills, TX

A practical order of operations for the first day, from stopping the water to getting your documentation in front of your insurer.

By the North Richland Hills Water Damage Pros team. Published October 26, 2026.

Start with the water, then the paperwork

A water damage insurance claim goes better when the damage is stopped before it is documented. Shut off the water at the main valve if a supply line failed, and keep people and pets out of any room where water has reached outlets or the electrical panel. If the water is from a sewer backup or an overflowing creek, treat it as contaminated and stay out of it.

Once the source is stopped and the house is safe, call your insurance agent or company. The Texas Department of Insurance tells homeowners to notify their insurer as early as possible, because policy deadlines vary and some require notice of water damage much sooner than the one-year window other policies allow.

Then call a restoration crew. Wet drywall, insulation and flooring start to break down within days, and a claim file that shows prompt drying looks very different from one that shows water sitting for a week. Our 24/7 line can dispatch an emergency crew to North Richland Hills while you wait on hold with your insurer.

Document the damage before anything is moved

Photos and video are the backbone of the claim. The Texas Department of Insurance advises taking them as soon as it is safe and holding on to damaged items until you have talked to your adjuster.

Work room by room and shoot wide first, then close. Capture the water line on walls, the ceiling stain from the room above, the floor and baseboards, and anything that sits in the water such as furniture, rugs or boxes in a garage.

  • Wide and close photos of every wet room, including the ceiling, walls, floor and baseboards.
  • A short video walk-through that narrates where the water came from and what it touched.
  • The failed part itself, such as a cracked supply line, a corroded fitting or a leaking water heater, kept in a bag if it is safe to handle.
  • A written list of damaged belongings with the age of each item and where it was bought, if you remember.
  • The date and time you first noticed the water, and when you shut off the source.

Do not throw out soaked carpet pad, drywall or contents until your insurer has seen them or has told you in writing that disposal is fine. Restoration crews take their own photos and moisture readings, which saves you from guessing at numbers later.

Which policy responds depends on where the water came from

Most disputes start with the cause of the water, not the amount of damage. A sudden and accidental event inside the home, such as a burst pipe, is often treated very differently from a slow leak that went unnoticed or from water that entered from outside. The table below is a decision framework, not a coverage promise, because only your policy language decides.

The Texas Department of Insurance states that most home and renters policies do not cover flood damage, although a few do. That makes it worth reading your declarations page before you need it.

What a restoration company adds to the file

Insurers want to see that the loss was measured and that the work matches the loss. A good restoration crew produces that paper trail as part of the job: moisture readings taken with meters, a sketch or list of the affected rooms, and logs that show the drying equipment running until the materials read dry.

Ask the company for a written scope of work before demolition starts, and ask how the scope and price will be explained to you. Our line explains scope and price before work starts, and the crew keeps readings you can hand to your adjuster.

Common mistakes that weaken a claim

Waiting several days to call is the most common one, since moisture damage grows and the insurer may argue that the extra damage came from delay. Another is repairing before inspection. If a plumber replaces a failed line and discards it, the evidence of what failed is gone.

People also over-promise on the cause. If you are not sure whether a leak was sudden or slow, say that plainly and let the adjuster and the restoration readings sort it out. A claim filed with a confident wrong guess is harder to fix than one filed with an honest "we do not know yet".

Finally, do not sign a contract or assignment of benefits form you have not read. Ask for a written scope and take time to compare it with what your insurer says it will cover.

North Richland Hills notes: water damage versus flood

North Richland Hills keeps a stormwater system that carries runoff to creeks and channels, and the city notes that homeowners inside and outside floodplains can have drainage problems. The city also says its flood maps show creek and channel flooding, not undersized drainage or poor lot grading, which can flood a yard or garage outside the mapped floodplain.

In practice, that means a map that shows your home outside the floodplain does not tell you whether a burst pipe or a rainwater backup is covered. Only your policy does. If you want to know whether your address sits in a mapped floodplain, the city offers an interactive map and a floodplain determination letter through Public Works Engineering.

If you do carry a flood policy, the Texas Department of Insurance warns that National Flood Insurance Program claims must be filed within 60 days. Check the date on your own policy and loss notice, because that deadline is far shorter than many homeowners expect.
