What a slab leak is and why water spreads the way it does

A slab leak is a leak in a pressurized water line that runs under or inside a concrete slab. You cannot see the pipe, so the first sign is usually water showing up somewhere else. Concrete is porous and slabs have joints, so water finds the low path and travels sideways before it surfaces.

That is why the wet spot may sit a room away from the broken pipe. It can rise into the bottom of a wall, soak the baseboard and slip under flooring. A plumber locates the break and repairs the line. We handle the water damage and drying.

Signs you may have a slab leak

  • A warm spot on the floor that you can feel with bare feet.
  • The sound of running water when every fixture is off.
  • Damp baseboards or a damp edge on carpet along one wall.
  • Flooring that lifts, cups or buckles with no obvious cause.
  • A water meter that keeps moving with everything off.

Ask your water utility how to read your meter, then check it with all fixtures off. A moving meter points to a leak somewhere between the meter and the house, and a plumber can narrow it down. None of these signs proves a slab leak alone.

Safety in the first hour of a slab leak

Act on the water first, then on the cause. Here is what to do before anyone arrives.

  • Shut off the main water valve for the house, and test that the sound of running water stops.
  • If water has reached an outlet, a light fixture or the breaker panel, keep the power off and stay out of the wet area.
  • Lift small rugs and electronics off the wet floor.
  • Take photos of the wet areas and the meter reading before anything moves.
  • Get a plumber to locate the leak, then ring (817) 623-9267 to start the floor drying.

How we dry a slab-on-grade house

Most of the damage sits in the finishes and the base of the walls, because the slab is the floor. We map moisture with a meter and a thermal camera, then pull wet baseboard, carpet and pad. Wet drywall at the wall base is cut back above the water line.

Hard flooring such as laminate and engineered wood often lifts and has to be removed so the slab can breathe. Air movers sweep the floor surface while LGR dehumidifiers take moisture out of the room air. We keep checking the slab and the wall bases until readings settle. Those calls follow the published ANSI/IICRC S500 standard.

We do not repair plumbing or foundations. If the plumber has to break into the slab, we coordinate drying around that work.

Insurance questions for a leak under the slab

The state insurance department separates a sudden and accidental release of water, often covered, from a continuous leak, which is excluded. A slab-damage endorsement is sold separately. A slab leak can sit on either side of those lines, and your insurer decides.

Ask what your policy says about the plumbing repair, the access to the pipe and the water damage separately. Keep the plumber report and photos of the damage.

Mistakes to avoid with a suspected slab leak

  • Waiting to see if the damp spot dries on its own.
  • Laying new flooring over a floor that has not been checked with a meter.
  • Assuming the wet wall is the leak location.
  • Leaving the main valve open while you wait for the plumber.

To prevent more damage, find the shut-off now and make sure everyone in the house knows where it is.
