Carpet Water Damage Drying and Cleanup in North Richland Hills, TX

Wet carpet looks like a surface problem, but the pad and subfloor underneath decide what happens next. Call (817) 623-9267 and we will help you sort out whether it can stay.

Carpet water damage: three layers, not one

Carpet is a system of three parts. The face fibers you walk on, the backing that holds them and the cushion, often called the pad, underneath. Below those sits a tack strip around the edge and a subfloor of wood or concrete.

The pad is the problem. It is built to be soft and porous, so it takes in water and holds it long after the surface feels merely damp. A damp pad stays in contact with the backing and the subfloor, which keeps moisture where drying air cannot reach it.

Whoever repairs the pipe or roof deals with the source, and our part is the wet carpet, the drying and the cleanup.

What you notice in wet carpet, and what it tells you

  • Squishing underfoot means the pad is saturated.
  • A musty or sour odor means the water has been there long enough to affect the pad and backing.
  • Brown or yellow stains rising from the bottom suggest dirt or tannin from the pad or subfloor is wicking up.
  • Color bleed along a seam or near a dyed item points to dye moving in wet fibers.
  • Ripples or a loose area suggest the stretch has failed or the backing is separating.

None of these alone makes the call. They tell us where to put the meter.

Wet carpet safety: the first hour

Go slowly. Wet carpet over a live floor can hide power cords and outlets.

  • Stop the water at its source. Close the valve or ask a plumber to.
  • Cut power to the room at the panel if water touched outlets or cords, provided you can get to the panel without stepping in water.
  • Do not walk on wet carpet more than necessary, and do not run a household vacuum on it.
  • Lift furniture off the carpet. If it must stay, place foil or plastic under the feet, because aluminum and wood legs can stain wet carpet.
  • Photograph the room, then call (817) 623-9267.

Can the carpet be dried in place, or does it have to be pulled?

The answer rests on the water and the time. Clean water caught quickly gives carpet the best chance, because the crew can extract it and dry the cushion and subfloor with airflow. Carpet that sat in water for a long time often loses that chance.

Water source matters too. The published ANSI/IICRC S500 standard groups water into categories by how contaminated it is. Clean supply water is the mildest group, and water from drains or outside sources is treated as more contaminated. Contaminated water usually means the pad and often the carpet are removed.

We do not state a number of days that carpet lasts. A meter reading and a look underneath settle it better than any rule of thumb.

Lifting a corner and what we find underneath

Carefully releasing a corner from the tack strip lets us see the pad and subfloor. We check the underside of the carpet, the pad for saturation and the subfloor for dampness and staining. This takes a few minutes and answers the biggest question.

If the carpet is clean and the water was clean, we usually pull the pad, which is cheap to replace, and dry the carpet and subfloor with the carpet lifted. Air movers push air under it and LGR dehumidifiers take the moisture out of the room. When the readings are right, the carpet is stretched and re-laid on a new pad.

Carpet that has delaminated, smells or has contaminated water on it is removed and disposed of. See water damage cleanup for how contaminated materials are handled.

Insurance, flood and wet carpet

Water from a burst pipe or appliance is a different insurance question from water that comes in from outside. Texas Department of Insurance flood tips warn that few home or renters policies pay for flood, and some do. They also say to take photos or video and keep damaged items until the adjuster has spoken with you. That includes the carpet.

We never promise coverage. Ask your insurer which events your policy covers.

Mistakes to avoid with wet carpet

  • Using a household vacuum on wet carpet.
  • Leaving furniture legs on wet carpet.
  • Throwing carpet away before the adjuster sees it.
  • Covering wet carpet with a rug or plastic.
  • Running a space heater near wet carpet.
