What a sewage backup is and where it shows up first

A sewage backup happens when wastewater cannot leave through the line and comes up through the lowest openings in the house. Floor drains, toilets and tubs are the usual first points. This page covers sewage inside the house. Water that rises from outside belongs on flood damage restoration.

The published ANSI/IICRC S500 standard sorts water into categories, and sewage falls in Category 3, meaning grossly contaminated. That category drives every decision that follows: what stays, what goes and how we protect the crew.

Signs that the problem is the sewer line

  • Several fixtures drain slowly or gurgle at the same time.
  • Water appears in a tub or shower when you flush or run a washer.
  • A floor drain overflows with dark water.
  • A strong sewage smell near the lowest drain in the house.

One slow sink usually means a local clog. Several fixtures acting up together points at the main line. A plumber can tell whether the blockage sits on your side or farther along.

Safety in the first hour of a sewage backup

Treat the water as contaminated. Keep out, and keep it contained.

  • Keep everyone out of the wet area, including children and pets.
  • Do not run water, flush toilets or use the washer or dishwasher.
  • Leave the power off if water has reached outlets or appliances.
  • Ask Public Works if the blockage is in the city main, using the Utilities Division number, 817-427-6440.
  • Call 911 if the situation is a safety risk, because the city page lists no after-hours number.
  • Get a plumber to clear the line, then ring (817) 623-9267 for the cleanup.

How we clean up after sewage

The plumber clears the line. We handle what the water touched. The crew wears protective gear and extracts the standing water with pumps and extraction units, then removes porous materials that absorbed it. Carpet, carpet pad, drywall and insulation are usually taken out and bagged.

Non-porous surfaces such as tile, concrete and sealed trim are cleaned and disinfected. Drying equipment then runs on the structure, and moisture meters tell us when it can stop. Odor control follows once the source materials are gone.

Photos of the affected rooms and removed materials are part of the job, because your insurer may ask for them.

  • Whether the water sat for hours or for days.
  • Protective gear, disposal and cleaning steps for the category.

Insurance and a sewer backup

The state insurance department guide describes sewer or drain backup as an optional endorsement and not standard coverage. Check yours for that wording. Flood coverage is a separate question.

Take photos before anything is removed and keep the plumber report. Hold onto damaged items until an adjuster has looked them over, which matches the state insurance department claim tips claim tips.

Mistakes to avoid with a sewage backup

  • Mopping or wet vacuuming the water yourself.
  • Running fans that spread contaminated mist through the house.
  • Flushing again to see if the line has cleared.
  • Keeping wet carpet pad or drywall because it looks fine.

To prevent a repeat, ask the plumber what caused the blockage.
