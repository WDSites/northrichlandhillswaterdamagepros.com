Kitchen Water Damage Services in North Richland Hills, TX

A kitchen packs cabinets, flooring, drywall and plumbing into one small room, and water finds the hidden parts first. Call (817) 623-9267 and we will talk through what to shut off and what to leave alone.

How kitchen water damage happens and why the room is tricky

Most kitchen water damage starts under the sink. A supply line weeps, a drain connection loosens or a dishwasher hose seeps for weeks before anyone looks. Because the cabinet door stays shut, the leak can run for a long time with nothing to see but a faint smell.

The room is tricky because every layer is different. Cabinet boxes sit on the floor, flooring runs under them, drywall sits behind them and the subfloor sits under everything. Water that enters at one point travels along the lowest path and soaks all of those layers at once.

A plumber repairs the line or the drain. We handle the water damage, drying and cleanup that follow. Appliance failures have their own page at appliance leak water damage, and a leaking tank is covered under water heater leak cleanup.

What you can see, and what is hiding behind it

Easy signs are a swollen toe-kick, a cabinet floor that feels soft, a countertop edge that lifts, flooring that cups or curls at a seam and a musty smell when you open the sink door. These tell you water has been present.

The hidden spots matter more. Water collects behind the dishwasher, under the sink base, inside the wall behind the range and in the cavity under a cabinet run. Those areas stay damp long after the visible surface dries, which is why a moisture meter and a thermal camera are used before anyone decides what is safe.

Kitchen water damage safety: the first hour

Kitchens mix water with outlets, appliances and heavy cabinets, so go in this order.

  • Close the shut-off valve under the sink. If it is stuck or the leak is behind a wall, close the main water shut-off.
  • Switch off the kitchen circuits at the panel when water has reached outlets, the range or the dishwasher, but only from dry footing.
  • Do not use the dishwasher or run the disposal until the plumbing is checked.
  • Take photos or video of the cabinets, floor and any damaged food or contents before moving anything.
  • A plumber stops the source. For the standing water and the drying, ring (817) 623-9267.

Can the cabinets be dried in place, or do they have to come out?

Cabinet material decides most of the answer. Plywood boxes tolerate moisture better and can often be dried with the toe-kick removed and air moved through the base. Particleboard boxes absorb water like a sponge, swell at the edges and lose their strength, so a soaked base often has to be replaced.

Laminate countertops are usually fine if the water stays below them, though the particleboard core can swell if it gets wet from the underside. Solid-surface tops are stable but may need to be lifted so the cabinet beneath can dry.

Cabinets that cover wet drywall or a wet subfloor sometimes have to be detached so the cavity can dry. We read the moisture first and then explain which pieces can stay.

Flooring, subfloor and the wall behind the cabinets

Flooring under cabinets reacts to water in different ways. Tile itself shrugs it off, but the grout, the underlayment and the subfloor below can hold moisture. Vinyl and laminate trap water at the seams and can delaminate. Hardwood cups as it absorbs moisture from below.

The subfloor is usually plywood or similar sheet material. It can often be dried, but a section that has softened or stayed wet for a long time may have to be cut out and replaced. Drywall behind cabinets and appliances is treated the same way, and our structural drying page explains how the drying itself works.

Insurance and kitchen leaks

The Texas Department of Insurance home insurance guide lists sudden and accidental release of water among the risks most policies cover, and lists a continuous water leak as an exclusion. A slow drip under the sink can look like the second kind, so document the date you found it.

The guide also says you might have different deductibles for each type of coverage. We never promise coverage. Your insurer decides, so keep damaged cabinet parts until the adjuster has seen them.

Mistakes to avoid in a wet kitchen

  • Running the dishwasher before the leak is traced.
  • Closing the cabinet doors on a wet base and hoping it dries.
  • Sealing wet cabinets in paint or caulk, which traps moisture.
  • Pulling cabinets tied to plumbing before asking the city building department.
  • Throwing out damaged items before taking photos.
