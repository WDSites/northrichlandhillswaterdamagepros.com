Why frozen pipes flood a house when it warms up

Water expands as it turns to ice. In a pipe, that pressure can split the wall of the pipe or pop a fitting, and the ice itself keeps the crack plugged. The house stays dry until the temperature climbs and the ice melts. Then the line, still under pressure, pours through the split.

That is why the call often comes hours after temperatures rise, not during the coldest night. Someone leaves for work with the house quiet and comes home to water in the ceiling or running under the garage door.

The places North Texas pipes freeze first

Pipes in exterior walls, attics, garages and crawl spaces lose heat fastest. So do outdoor hose bibs and sprinkler lines, which can crack even if the rest of the house stays warm. A pipe on the north side of the house, or one where insulation was never installed or has slumped, is a likely candidate.

A hose bib that split at the wall can send water into the cavity behind the siding, not onto the ground. A sprinkler line that cracked underground may only show as a soggy yard, or as water finding its way to the foundation edge.

Frozen pipe safety: what to do before and after the thaw

If you think a pipe is frozen but nothing has leaked yet, you have a window to prevent the flood. If water is already flowing, act on the shut-off first.

  • Shut the main before the thaw if a pipe is suspected frozen or split.
  • Do not use open flame or a torch on a pipe, and do not stand in water near electrical outlets.
  • If power is out, leave electronics and panel work to an electrician. A freeze can interrupt power and make the house dark and hard to assess.
  • Open the faucets after the repair to let air out and refill slowly.
  • Call a plumber for the pipe and call us for the wet walls, ceilings and floors.

Our drying process after a freeze break

We do not repair pipes. Your plumber replaces the split section and restores service. We take over the wet house, starting with moisture meters and a thermal camera to map what the thaw soaked, including attic insulation and the base of exterior walls.

Drying uses air movers, LGR dehumidifiers and, where needed, extraction units to clear standing water. Insulation that is soaked is usually removed, because it holds water against framing. Cost after a freeze: the usual drivers

  • How many pipes split and how many rooms the thaw reached.
  • Whether the break was in an attic, where insulation soaks up water.
  • The amount of drywall and flooring that cannot be dried in place.
  • Drying days and equipment, set by moisture readings.
  • Delays between the thaw and the first call.

When many homes need help at once, crew schedules also come into play, which is why an early call matters.

Freeze damage and your insurance policy

The Texas Department of Insurance home guide separates sudden and accidental water release from a continuous leak. A pipe that split and then flooded at thaw sits near the first. A pipe that dripped for weeks beforehand may be treated differently. Your insurer decides, so read the policy and ask.

Take photos, keep what the water ruined and tell your insurer early, as the TDI damage tips suggest. Water categories are defined in ANSI/IICRC S500, a published standard.

Freeze mistakes to avoid and how to prevent a repeat

  • Leaving a suspect frozen line alone while you wait for it to thaw on its own.
  • Running a heater or torch on a pipe.
  • Skipping the attic after a freeze because the ceiling looks dry.
  • Forgetting hose bibs and sprinkler lines when you prepare for a cold night.
