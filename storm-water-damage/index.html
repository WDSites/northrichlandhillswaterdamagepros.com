How storm water damage gets into a home

Storm water damage is water that enters a house because weather opened a path for it. Wind-driven rain can push past window frames and door thresholds that stay dry on a calm day. Hail can break window glass. A fallen tree or limb can punch through a roof or wall and leave an opening the size of a doorway.

Drainage plays a part too. Gutters packed with leaves and debris overflow along the wall, and water that cannot leave the yard fast enough can find its way to a door or a low spot in the foundation line. The common thread is that a storm rarely makes just one opening, so the wet areas are often spread through the house.

What you see after a storm and what it means

Start with the obvious signs. A ceiling stain that grows, a wet strip of carpet along an exterior wall, bubbling paint under a window and a damp smell in a closet all point to water that kept moving after the rain stopped.

Look for a pattern. Wet carpet at one window and a stained ceiling in a bedroom down the hall tell you there are at least two paths, and each needs its own check. Water travels along framing and under flooring, so a dry-looking room next to a wet one is not proof it escaped.

  • Wet carpet or pad along exterior walls often means wind-driven rain at a window or door.
  • Broken glass and soaked curtains point to hail or debris at the window.
  • A ceiling stain with a soft or sagging spot means water is sitting in the drywall above you.
  • Water pooled at a threshold after heavy rain may have come from the yard side.

First hour safety steps after a storm

Storms often take out power, and that changes the risk. Treat every wet room as unsafe for electrical equipment until the power is confirmed off. Then work through the steps below.

  • Stay out of rooms where the ceiling sags or ripples, because wet drywall can fall.
  • If water is near outlets, appliances or the panel, leave the power off and do not stand in standing water to reach a switch.
  • Keep clear of downed lines or a limb lying across a wire, and call 911 for any immediate danger.
  • Move people and pets to a dry room, and lift small items and electronics out of the wet.
  • Take photos or video of the damage and the opening first, then start moving things.

Our process: we extract and dry, a roofer or contractor closes the opening

We do not repair roofs, windows or walls. A roofer or contractor handles tarping, boarding up and rebuilding the opening, and we handle the water. Getting the opening covered first matters, because drying a house that is still taking in rain does not work.

On arrival we start with a moisture survey using meters and a thermal camera across every room that may be affected. Extraction units pull standing water from floors, and wet pad and unsalvageable materials are removed. Air movers and LGR dehumidifiers then dry the structure, with readings repeated until the framing reaches its drying goal. Antimicrobial treatment is used where the water source or conditions call for it.

The work follows the published standard ANSI/IICRC S500 as a reference for water damage restoration. For the equipment and monitoring side, see structural drying. For what the first days look like, read water mitigation.

What changes the cost of storm water damage

  • How many rooms took water, since storms rarely wet just one.
  • Whether the water came through the roof, a window or a wall, and what it soaked on the way.
  • How long water sat before extraction began and whether the opening is covered.
  • The materials involved, such as carpet and pad, drywall, insulation or flooring.
  • How many days of drying equipment and monitoring the structure needs.

Storm water damage and insurance: wind versus flood

Coverage for storm damage depends on how the water got in. The Texas Department of Insurance home insurance guide says most home policies do not cover damage caused by floods, and most flood policies have a 30-day waiting period before they take effect. Water that came through a wind-damaged roof or a hail-broken window is a different question from water that rose outside and came in.

TDI flood tips say NFIP flood policies require filing a claim within 60 days. Photos and video taken as soon as it is safe, and damaged items kept until your adjuster has seen them, help any claim. Coverage is never promised here, so read your policy and put the question to your insurer. If outside water rose into the home, see flood damage restoration.

Mistakes to avoid after a storm

  • Turning the power back on in a wet room before it has been checked.
  • Waiting on drying until the roofer arrives, when the interior can be extracted in the meantime.
  • Throwing out damaged items before photos and before your adjuster has seen them.
  • Treating one stain as the whole problem and missing the second opening.
  • Assuming a roof path is the only source. A roof-only leak is covered on roof leak water damage.
