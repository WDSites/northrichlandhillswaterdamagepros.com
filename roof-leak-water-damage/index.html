The path rain takes from the roof into the house

Rain enters through a lifted shingle, a cracked flashing joint or a hole left by hail or wind. It runs down the roof deck, soaks the attic insulation and settles onto the ceiling drywall. The stain you see below can be several feet from the opening above.

This page is about water that came through the roof. Whole-event wind and rain intrusion is covered on storm water damage. A stain that grows only when the air conditioner runs is a different cause, explained on AC leak water damage.

Signs the roof is the source

  • Stains that spread after a storm and hold still on dry days.
  • A bowed, bulging or sagging ceiling.
  • Drips from a light fixture or vent during rain.
  • Wet, flattened insulation if you can see into the attic.
  • Peeling paint or a damp smell in a top-floor room.

A ceiling that sags is holding water. Keep people and pets out from under it and do not poke it to test.

Safety in the first hour of a roof leak

Rain is still coming, so the steps focus on keeping people safe and slowing the damage.

  • Stay out from under any bowed or sagging ceiling.
  • Switch off power to wet rooms if water reaches fixtures, outlets or the attic.
  • Place buckets under drips and cover furniture with plastic.
  • Do not climb onto a wet roof or into a wet attic.
  • Get a roofer to repair or tarp the roof, then ring (817) 623-9267 for the water inside.

How we extract and dry after a roof leak

The roofer stops the entry. We take care of the water already inside. A moisture meter and a thermal camera map the wet insulation, ceiling and wall tops. Standing water is extracted, and saturated attic insulation is removed.

Wet drywall that has softened or sagged is cut out back to dry board. Fans and LGR dehumidifiers work on whatever ceiling and framing remain. If the attic held wiring in the wet area, we flag it and tell you to have an electrician look before the power is restored there.

Sorting wet materials follows the published ANSI/IICRC S500 standard. Ceiling rebuild belongs to water damage repair.

What affects the cost of roof leak cleanup

  • Hours or days of rain before the roof was covered.
  • How much attic insulation and ceiling drywall is wet.
  • Ceiling height and attic access.
  • The number of rooms below the opening.
  • Drying days needed until readings return to normal.

Insurance, hail and roof leaks

Insurers often treat sudden storm damage differently from wear and long-term leaks. The Texas Department of Insurance guide warns that each type of coverage on a policy can carry its own deductible, and its roof example refers to replacement cost. Read your policy for how the roof and the interior damage are handled.

Photograph the ceiling, the attic insulation and any visible roof damage when it is safe. Keep the roofer invoice and tarp receipt.

Mistakes to avoid after a roof leak

  • Waiting for a dry day before calling anyone.
  • Poking a bulging ceiling.
  • Painting over the stain while the insulation is still wet.
  • Climbing onto a wet roof to look for the hole.

To prevent more damage, get the roof covered first and the wet materials out soon after.
