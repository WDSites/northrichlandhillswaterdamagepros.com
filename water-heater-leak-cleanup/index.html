Tank failure or slow drip: two different problems

Water heater leaks come in two forms. A fitting, a drain valve or a temperature and pressure relief valve can drip slowly for a long time. A tank that corrodes through can fail suddenly and release whatever water is inside it, plus the supply behind it until someone closes the valve.

The slow version is easy to ignore, and that is the problem. A damp spot under a tank rarely stays small, because the water has a flat floor to travel across and a wall base to wick into. The sudden version is obvious, but the volume is what makes it costly.

Where the tank sits decides what gets wet

Heaters sit in closets, garages, utility rooms and attics. Where the tank sits determines the damage. A closet unit wets the wall base and flooring in the adjacent hall or bedroom. A garage tank sends water across the slab and into the bottom of walls. An attic tank soaks insulation and loads the ceiling below until it sags.

Tanks above living space deserve a second look. A small leak in an attic can run for a long time before a stain appears on a ceiling.

Water heater safety: the first hour

Hot water can scald, and the leak may be near a power or gas connection. Here is what to do, in order.

  • Turn off the electric breaker to the heater or set a gas unit to its off or pilot position.
  • Close the cold water supply valve on the pipe above the tank.
  • Stay clear of water that came from the tank, which can be very hot, and keep children and pets away.
  • If you smell gas, leave the house and call the gas utility or 911 from outside.
  • Call a plumber to replace the unit and call us for the water damage.

What the plumber replaces and what we dry

The plumber replaces the heater, valves, pan and drain line. We do not repair or replace plumbing or appliances. Our work is the structure around the unit.

We use moisture meters and a thermal camera to find wet wall base, flooring, subfloor and insulation. Extraction equipment clears standing water first, then air movers and LGR dehumidifiers take over. Soaked baseboards, insulation and drywall are cut out where they cannot be dried. A slow leak under the tank becomes subfloor and wall damage

A weeping valve can seem too small to bother with. Over time the moisture goes into the flooring, the wall base and the subfloor beneath. Particleboard and drywall paper soften, trim swells, and the area smells musty. By then it is a drying and repair job, not a plumbing adjustment.

A drain pan with a drain line carries small leaks away, but only if the line is open. If you see rust, damp or stains around the pan, have the unit checked before the leak turns into a full failure.

Heater leaks and insurance

A tank that fails suddenly may fit the sudden and accidental category in the TDI guide, while a long weeping valve may look like a continuous leak. Your insurer makes that call. Deductibles may differ by coverage type, so read your policy.

Photograph the unit, the pan and the wet areas, and keep the failed parts if your plumber allows. ANSI/IICRC S500 is the published standard used to classify the water.

Mistakes to avoid with a leaking water heater

  • Keeping the heater on while it leaks.
  • Touching hot water or a wet electrical connection.
  • Mopping up and ignoring the wall base and subfloor.
  • Leaving a damp closet closed up with the door shut.
